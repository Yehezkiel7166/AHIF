# Causal Reasoning Protocol

## Principle

AHIF decisions must be consequences of context, identity, human behavior, and visual logic. A decision is valid only when its causal path is explicit and does not conflict with a higher-priority invariant.

## Canonical chain

```text
Premise
→ Context interpretation
→ Applicable constraints
→ Candidate decision
→ Cross-domain effects
→ Accepted decision
→ Compiler directive
```

## Example

```text
Premise: cold, windy morning in an outdoor waterfront location
Context interpretation: thermal protection and wind response are required
Constraints: identity visibility, realistic movement, refined character style
Candidate decision: structured long coat with controlled hair movement
Cross-domain effects: hands may secure the coat; camera should retain facial readability
Accepted decision: long coat, subtle wind-reactive hair, natural protective gesture
Compiler directive: describe practical cold-weather styling and physically plausible wind response
```

## Causal validity rules

1. A decision must cite at least one contextual premise.
2. Identity-sensitive decisions must cite an identity invariant.
3. Cross-domain consequences must be recorded when a decision affects another domain.
4. Aesthetic preference cannot be the sole justification for a realism-sensitive decision.
5. Missing input must not be converted into a false fact.
6. Contradictory causal chains must be resolved before compilation.

## Invalid reasoning patterns

- choosing a heavy coat in hot weather only because it looks luxurious
- selecting a wide-angle close portrait without accounting for facial distortion
- adding culturally specific clothing without location or activity support
- assigning a dramatic expression that contradicts the requested calm atmosphere
- obscuring the face when identity preservation is the primary objective
